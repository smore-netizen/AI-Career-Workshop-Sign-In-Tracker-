Rappi · Buenos Aires · April 2026
Choosing Rappi's next market: an IPO-readiness strategy
The challenge

Rappi, Latin America's delivery super-app, had dropped from a $5.5B valuation in 2021 to about $1.8B. Heading toward a potential IPO, it needed a credible growth story and a clear answer to where post-IPO capital should go.

What I did

On the ground in Buenos Aires, our three-person team interviewed a local restaurant partner and a former UBS markets analyst, then analyzed Rappi's competitive position, its AI opportunity, and IPO comparables like Mercado Libre, DoorDash, and Grab. I led the overall strategic recommendation: where Rappi should expand once it goes public.

The result

We recommended using post-IPO capital to pilot entry into Panama City, for three reasons: one of the highest GDPs per capita in Latin America for Rappi's higher-margin services, a dollarized economy that offsets currency risk from markets like Argentina, and the urban density Rappi needs to operate profitably. Rappi's fintech and travel services also give it a clear niche against PedidosYa and Uber Eats there.

Independent Artist · Growth
Growing an audience from zero to 130K+ views on a $200 budget
The challenge

Launching as an independent artist with no audience, no label, and almost no marketing budget.

What I did

Started on BandLab, where I reached 1,700 followers in two months, then moved my audience to Instagram. I studied which trending videos performed best and replicated what worked, then used my analytics to find the days and times my audience was most active and posted daily on that schedule. Whenever a video organically passed 10,000 views, I put a small ad budget behind it to amplify what was already working.

The result

3,100+ followers and 130,000+ video views, with one clip reaching 50,000 views, on $200 of total ad spend. My original singing videos consistently performed best. The same playbook I bring to GTM: find the signal, double down, and only spend where it's already working.
